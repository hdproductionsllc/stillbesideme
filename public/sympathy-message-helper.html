Questions you might have

What should I say to someone who lost a pet?

Acknowledge their loss directly. Say the pet's name. Avoid minimizing phrases like "it was just a pet." Something like "I'm so sorry about [name]. They were so lucky to have you" goes a long way. Our free tool above can help you find the right words. If you want to send a meaningful gift alongside your message, explore our pet memorial gifts.

What do you write in a sympathy card?

Keep it simple and sincere. Mention the person or pet by name, share a brief memory if you have one, and let them know you're thinking of them. Avoid clichés like "they're in a better place." Our message generator can help you draft something heartfelt. For more guidance, read our blog post on how to write a sympathy card.

Is it okay to send a text instead of a card?

Yes. What matters is that you reach out. A thoughtful text message is always better than silence. You can follow up later with a card, gift, or visit. If you'd like to send something more lasting, browse our sympathy gifts.

What should I NOT say to someone who is grieving?

Avoid "everything happens for a reason," "at least they lived a long life," "you can get another pet," or "I know how you feel." These phrases minimize the pain. Instead, simply acknowledge their loss and offer your presence. Use the tool above to generate a message that strikes the right tone.
