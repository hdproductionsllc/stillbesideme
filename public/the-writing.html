The Writing

We don't write about grief.
We write the pet back into the room.

Every tribute is a photograph and a poem. The photograph shows you the animal. The poem does the harder thing. It puts down the small ordinary details that made them who they were. Read one below, the way it hangs on the wall.

Recognizable, not interchangeable.

Most memorial writing stays general. The loyal friend, the hole in the heart, the bridge. Ours goes the other way. It reaches for the one flat grey tennis ball, the truck heard three streets away, the exact spot by the door. A stranger could pick your dog out of a crowd from the poem alone.

The line that undoes you is the one still in the room.

The hardest lines aren't about dying. They're about the ordinary thing left behind, still sitting there, still waiting, as if nothing had changed.

The blue chair is still yours. We just sit beside it now.Pixie
The ball's still on the porch. We couldn't bring ourselves to move it.Rusty
I still wake at six. I still reach across the pillow.Marbles
The house is bigger without you.Callie
Slow down now, sweet one. We will catch up to you.Biscuit

First, it makes you smile.

Before the turn, there is almost always a small joke. The pet caught being exactly, ridiculously themselves. That's what makes them feel alive on the page, so the ache that follows lands on someone real instead of a symbol.

“Head on the pillow like you paid the mortgage.”Marbles
“…and found it, on the whole, acceptable.”Tigger
“Turned up at the door at six on the dot. Dinner, right on time.”Ziggy

A wall of them. No two could be swapped.

Each tribute is written from scratch for one animal, with its own habits, its own room, its own small joke. Hang a dozen together and the writing never repeats itself.

From the families.

Some of these went home to real families. Here is what they wrote back.

“Slow down now, sweet one. We will catch up to you.”

A photograph freezes a moment. A tribute like this gives the whole animal back: the mischief, the routine, the particular way the house feels without them.

That balance is the product.

The specific detail. The small joke. The thing left on the porch. That is what separates this from sentimental, interchangeable memorial poetry, and it is why it stays on the wall instead of in a drawer.

Their story is kept. Their room stays theirs.
